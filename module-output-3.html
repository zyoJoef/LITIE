My Digital Safety and Privacy Profile

A Personal Cybersecurity and Ethics Assessment

This module output evaluates my personal cybersecurity habits, privacy practices, and responsible use of technology. It helps me identify possible risks and create practical ways to become a safer and more responsible digital citizen.

Part 1: Personal Cybersecurity Checkup

I actively use several online accounts for communication, education, entertainment, shopping, and other daily activities. I also use different devices such as a smartphone, laptop, and other internet-connected devices.

My Digital Environment

Active Online Accounts: Around 5 or more

Main Devices: Smartphone and laptop

Security Measures: Screen lock, passwords, software updates, antivirus/security protection, and MFA on some accounts

Possible Risky Habits

  • Clicking links without verifiying it's authenticity
  • Using the same or weak password pattern on some accounts
  • Connecting to unsafe public Wi-Fi
  • Accepting app permissions without reviewing them
  • Sharing too much information on social media

Reflection

The cyber threats that could most likely to affect me are phishing, account theft, malware, scams, and unauthorized access. I have encountered suspicious messages sent via SMS and links that appeared to be promotions, account warnings, and or other urgent requests.

My email, social media, school, shopping, and financial-related accounts would take the most damage/impact if compromised because they may contain personal information and could be used for other services. This showed me that protecting one important account can also help protect other parts of my digital life.

Part 2: Phishing Awareness Investigation

I reviewed examples of suspicious messages that a person may encounter online. These examples show how scammers use urgency, fake offers, and requests for information to deceive users.

# Suspicious Message Red Flags Type of Attack Targeted Information Possible Consequences
1 "Your account will be deactivated. Click this link to verify your account." Urgent message, suspicious link, and threat of account deactivation. Phishing Username, password, and account information. Account theft or unauthorized access.
2 "Congratulations! You won a special online shopping reward. Claim it now." Unexpected reward, unrealistic offer, and request to click a link. Scam / Phishing Personal information and payment information. Financial loss or identity theft.
3 "We are hiring immediately. Send your personal information and pay a small processing fee." Unverified job offer, payment request, and collection of personal information. Fake Job Scam Personal information and money. Financial loss and misuse of personal data.

Phishing Reflection

The main lesson I learned from these examples is that suspicious messages often try to make people react quickly without thinking. I should check the sender, inspect links carefully, avoid giving sensitive information through messages, and verify requests using official websites or applications.

Part 3: Password and Account Security Audit

Password security is important because one compromised password can potentially expose multiple accounts when passwords are reused. I reviewed my password practices without revealing any actual passwords.

Password Uniqueness

I am working toward using different passwords for my important accounts instead of reusing the same password.

Password Length

I should use longer passwords or passphrases that are difficult for other people to guess.

Multi-Factor Authentication

I use MFA on some important accounts and should continue enabling it on other accounts that support it.

Password Manager

A password manager can help store unique passwords and reduce the need to reuse passwords.

Security Reflection

My strongest protection should be on accounts containing important personal, school, communication, or financial information. Accounts without MFA or accounts using weak or repeated passwords need improvement.

Reusing passwords creates a major risk because if one website experiences a data breach and the password is exposed, attackers may try the same password on other services. Using unique passwords and MFA can reduce this risk.

Part 4: Personal Cyber Hygiene Scorecard

I rated my current cybersecurity habits from 1 to 5, where 1 means I need major improvement and 5 means I consistently practice the behavior.

Cyber Hygiene Practice Rating Assessment
Updating software regularly 4/5 I usually install important updates.
Backing up files 3/5 I need to become more consistent with backups.
Reviewing app permissions 3/5 I sometimes check permissions but can improve.
Using antivirus/security tools 4/5 I keep security protection enabled.
Avoiding suspicious links 4/5 I try to verify links before opening them.
Securing Wi-Fi connections 4/5 I use trusted and password-protected networks.
Using MFA 3/5 I use MFA on some important accounts.
Monitoring privacy settings 3/5 I need to review privacy settings more regularly.

Analysis

My strongest habits are avoiding suspicious links, keeping software updated, and using security protection. My weaker areas are backups, reviewing app permissions, MFA coverage, and regular privacy-setting reviews.

The areas that require immediate improvement are enabling MFA on important accounts, creating regular backups, and checking which applications have access to my information.

Part 6: Privacy and Data Protection Reflection

I share some personal information online when creating accounts, communicating with others, studying, shopping, or using digital services. This may include my name, email address, phone number, profile information, photos, and information needed to use a particular service.

I do not always read privacy policies completely because they can be long and difficult to understand. However, I have learned that privacy policies are important because they explain how companies may collect, use, store, and share my personal information.

I am becoming more careful about companies collecting my data. The convenience of online services is useful, but I also need to consider what information I am giving in exchange for that convenience.

Reflection Questions

I think many people do not fully understand how their data is collected and used because they often accept terms and privacy notices without reading them carefully. People may focus more on getting access to a service than understanding the information they are sharing.

I would never intentionally share passwords, authentication codes, banking credentials, or highly sensitive personal information publicly online. I also need to be careful when sharing personal photos, locations, school information, and other details that could be misused.

Part 7: Digital Safety and Responsibility Action Plan

Cybersecurity Goals

  1. Enable MFA on all major accounts that support it.
  2. Use unique and strong passwords for important accounts.
  3. Create regular backups of important school and personal files.

Privacy Goals

  1. Review privacy settings at least once a month.
  2. Reduce unnecessary sharing of personal information online.
  3. Review app permissions and remove unnecessary access.

Ethical Technology Goals

  1. Verify the legality of materials before downloading or sharing them.
  2. Respect copyright and intellectual property.
  3. Respect other people's privacy and information when using digital platforms.

My Commitment

I will apply these goals in my everyday use of technology. Instead of only reacting when a security problem happens, I will practice prevention by protecting my accounts, controlling the information I share, and making ethical decisions online.

Final Reflection

After studying cybersecurity, privacy, and digital ethics, I have become more aware that using technology also means taking responsibility for my safety and the information I handle. Cybersecurity risks such as phishing, malware, scams, weak passwords, and unauthorized access are now more important to me because they can affect my accounts, personal information, school activities, and finances.

I also understand that privacy is important even when digital services make life more convenient. I need to think carefully about what information I share, what permissions I give to apps, and how companies may use my data. At the same time, ethical technology use means respecting other people's privacy, intellectual property, and information.

In the future, I will improve my digital habits by using stronger account security, enabling MFA, avoiding suspicious links, reviewing privacy settings, backing up important files, and thinking before sharing information online. These lessons will help me become a more careful, responsible, and security-conscious digital citizen.
